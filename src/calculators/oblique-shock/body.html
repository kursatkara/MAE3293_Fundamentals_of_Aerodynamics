Relations used

The θ–β–M relation

\[ \tan\theta = 2\cot\beta\;\frac{M_1^2\sin^2\beta-1}{M_1^2\left(\gamma+\cos2\beta\right)+2} \tag{9.23} \]

For a given M1, θ rises from 0 at the Mach angle μ = sin−1(1/M1) to a maximum θmax and falls back to 0 at β = 90°, the normal shock. Every θ < θmax therefore has two solutions: the weak one (smaller β, usually observed on wedges and corners) and the strong one. For θ > θmax no attached shock exists and the shock detaches. The calculator finds β, or M1, by bisection on (9.23).

Across the shock

\[ M_{n,1}=M_1\sin\beta \tag{9.13} \]
\[ M_{n,2}^2=\frac{1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_{n,1}^2}{\gamma M_{n,1}^2-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}} \tag{9.14} \]
\[ \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}=\frac{(\gamma+1)M_{n,1}^2}{2+(\gamma-1)M_{n,1}^2},\qquad \frac{p_2}{p_1}=1+\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(M_{n,1}^2-1\right) \]
\[ \frac{T_2}{T_1}=\frac{p_2}{p_1}\,\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2},\qquad M_2=\frac{M_{n,2}}{\sin(\beta-\theta)} \tag{9.18} \]

These are the normal-shock relations (9.15)–(9.17) applied to Mn,1; the tangential velocity is unchanged across the shock.
