The second turns a supersonic flow through θ isentropically, either as an expansion around a convex corner, where ν(M2) = ν(M1) + θ, or as a gradual isentropic compression on a concave wall, where ν(M2) = ν(M1) − θ, and gives the pressure, density and temperature ratios. Relations used

Prandtl–Meyer function

\[ \nu(M)=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}}\tan^{-1}\!\sqrt{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}\left(M^2-1\right)}-\tan^{-1}\!\sqrt{M^2-1} \]
\[ \theta=\nu(M_2)-\nu(M_1),\qquad \mu=\sin^{-1}\frac{1}{M} \]

ν rises monotonically from 0 at M = 1 toward νmax = (π/2)(√((γ+1)/(γ−1)) − 1) as M → ∞ (130.45° for γ = 1.4), so a turn larger than νmax − ν(M1) would expand the flow to zero pressure. The calculator inverts ν by bisection.

Across the turn

\[ p_{0,2}=p_{0,1},\qquad T_{0,2}=T_{0,1} \]
\[ \frac{T_2}{T_1}=\frac{1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_1^2}{1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_2^2},\quad \frac{p_2}{p_1}=\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}},\quad \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}=\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}} \]

The turn is isentropic, so the total conditions are unchanged and the static ratios follow from the isentropic relations at M1 and M2. For a compression θ enters with the opposite sign: ν(M2) = ν(M1) − θ, so M2 < M1 and p2 > p1. Since ν(1) = 0, a compression can turn the flow through at most ν(M1) before it reaches M = 1.

The compression is isentropic only if the wall turns gradually. Its Mach waves converge and coalesce into an oblique shock away from the wall; at a sharp concave corner the shock forms at the wall itself, and the Oblique Shock calculator applies.
