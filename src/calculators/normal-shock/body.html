Relations used

Across the shock

\[ M_2^2=\frac{1+\frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_1^2}{\gamma M_1^2-\frac{\gamma-1}{2}} \tag{8.59} \]
\[ \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}=\frac{u_1}{u_2}=\frac{(\gamma+1)M_1^2}{2+(\gamma-1)M_1^2} \tag{8.61} \]
\[ \frac{p_2}{p_1}=1+\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}\left(M_1^2-1\right) \tag{8.65} \]
\[ \frac{T_2}{T_1}=\frac{p_2}{p_1}\,\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2} \tag{8.67} \]

Total pressure, entropy, and the Pitot tube

\[ T_{0,2}=T_{0,1},\qquad \frac{p_{0,2}}{p_{0,1}}=e^{-(s_2-s_1)/R} \]
\[ \frac{p_{0,2}}{p_1}=\left[\frac{(\gamma+1)^2M_1^2}{4\gamma M_1^2-2(\gamma-1)}\right]^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}\frac{1-\gamma+2\gamma M_1^2}{\gamma+1} \tag{8.80} \]

Every ratio is monotonic in M1 for M1 ≥ 1, so a known ratio fixes M1; the calculator finds it by bisection on 1 ≤ M1 ≤ 50. The Rayleigh Pitot formula (8.80) gives the pressure at the nose of a Pitot tube in supersonic flow, behind the normal part of its bow shock.
